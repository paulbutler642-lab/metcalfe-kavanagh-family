What the file establishes

The file contains William’s signed voluntary-levy form dated 14 July 1922 and his signed Irish Republican Army Volunteer Reserve Agreement dated 16 July 1922. The Agreement carries the acceptance marking “Accepted. Curragh Camp.” His name is faint but legible on the signature line. Together, these papers directly document his signed and accepted enrolment in the Volunteer Reserve. Read alongside his National Army Census entry, they place him on the pro-Treaty side of the Irish Civil War.

Later pages record National Forces service and attestations, service administration, medical and dental paperwork, and the end of his engagement in November 1933. Faint handwriting and conflicting entries are identified as such rather than silently resolved.

Historical significance

The records verify William’s service with the pro-Treaty National Army during the Civil War. The war began on 28 June 1922, when the National Army attacked the anti-Treaty occupation of the Four Courts. The Defence Forces’ official history identifies the National Army as the pro-Treaty IRA. RTÉ’s account of the Army’s formation records that Michael Collins became its Commander-in-Chief on 12 July 1922. William signed his levy form on 14 July; his Volunteer Reserve Agreement was accepted at Curragh Camp on 16 July, four days after Collins took command. The November National Army Census records William as a Corporal in the Infantry and gives his attestation date as 12 July. Together, these records place William in the pro-Treaty forces under Collins’s overall command.

The acceptance stamp links William’s service to Curragh Camp, which had been handed over to Free State forces on 16 May 1922, as documented in the Military Archives. The papers do not show that Collins personally received William’s form or that the two met. The form says the Government called the voluntary levy to deal with a “National Emergency” and offers a six-month term, or a shorter period determined by the Army Council.

The form’s “I.R.A. Record” table is blank. It does not establish that William was a pre-Treaty IRA veteran. It separately records about four and a half years’ previous British Army service. The six-month agreement is an offer of service, not evidence that he completed only six months; later service papers show his Army service continued to 1933.

Historical references: Defence Forces — History of the Army · RTÉ — Collins and the creation of the National Army · Military Archives — Michael Collins, Commander-in-Chief · Military Archives — Curragh takeover, 16 May 1922 · Military Archives — National Army Census history.

Reading the dates and address: The handwritten dates 14 and 16 July 1922 are faint but legible. The 14 July levy form reads 59 Brookfield Buildings; the faint address on the 16 July Agreement appears to read 57 Brookfield Buildings. The forms prove his enrolment by those dates, but do not independently establish when he first became a volunteer or his status on the date of the February 1922 robbery report.

Which side was which in 1922?

Pro-Treaty: supported the Anglo-Irish Treaty and the Provisional Government; its forces became known as the National Army.

Anti-Treaty: rejected the Treaty as falling short of the Republic they had fought for, including because of its oath to the British monarch. They continued as the Anti-Treaty IRA; the Provisional Government and pro-government press often called them “Irregulars”.

Pro-Treaty supporters argued the Treaty offered a step towards self-government and avoided renewed war with Britain. Both sides came from the IRA before the split, so “IRA” on a 1922 document does not by itself identify which side it refers to. William’s form uses the title “Irish Republican Army Volunteer Reserve”; read together with his accepted Curragh papers and the National Army Census entry, it confirms his service on the pro-Treaty side.

Pages 19–21 · Agreement and personnel correspondence

Page 20 contains Personnel Records Section correspondence dated 16 October 1933 about the expiry of William’s engagement. Page 21 is the Irish Republican Army Volunteer Reserve Agreement. William’s faint signature appears on the service-offer line, the handwritten date reads 16 July 1922, and the form is marked “Accepted. Curragh Camp.” The address is faint and appears to read 57 Brookfield Buildings, Blackrock. The form lists a printed daily rate of 2 shillings and 6 pence plus maintenance, but a handwritten alteration above “Two” appears to read “Three”, so the rate actually applied is uncertain. Its dependant schedule lists 4 shillings per day for a wife; William’s handwritten note appears to direct payment of the allowance to his wife. The form records an allowance schedule and request, not proof of a payment made.

Pages 22–23 · National Forces attestation and oath, 1924

The signed attestation is dated 26 April 1924 and identifies William with the 27th Battalion. Its accompanying oath records service from 1 April 1924 and appears to give 30 September 1928 as the end date. The handwritten birth information on this later paperwork conflicts with the 1896 civil birth record.

Pages 31–36 · Later attestation and oath, November 1926

A further signed attestation names William Metcalfe and the 27th Battalion. The oath and recruiting certification are dated 12 November 1926. Read alongside the 1924 pages, these papers show continued service documentation; they are not evidence of a fresh IRA Reserve enrolment in 1926.

Pages 37–50 · Service extensions, conduct and discharge, 1931–1933

The later section includes extension forms and related battalion correspondence. These later extension papers, rather than the July 1922 Agreement itself, show that William continued in Army service beyond the initial six-month offer. A letter dated 6 October 1933 says he would not be permitted to extend his original enlistment. Personnel Records Section correspondence dated 16 October 1933 lists his term ending on 18 November 1933. The file records the end of this engagement in 1933.

Related record outside this file

National Army Census, 1922

The National Army Census was taken on 12–13 November 1922. The family transcription lists William as a Corporal in the Infantry and records his attestation date as 12 July 1922 at Dundalk, with paybook number 19818. The July date is the attestation date, not the census date. This census is separate from the 50-page personnel file above. See the Military Archives history of the census.
